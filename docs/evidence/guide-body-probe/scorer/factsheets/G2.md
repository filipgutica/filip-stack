1. Usable capacity is a tram's total parcel spaces minus its reserved spaces, and only usable spaces are available for ordinary parcels.
2. Queue ratio is the number of waiting ordinary parcels divided by usable capacity, with a ratio of one meaning that the queue exactly fills the available spaces.
3. Balanced transfer moves waiting parcels between eligible trams to reduce the larger queue ratio and stops when the ratios are equal or no permitted whole-parcel move would reduce the larger ratio.
4. `[agent-only]` The dispatch simulator stores queue ratios to six decimal places.
5. A queue ratio above one means that some waiting parcels cannot fit on that tram's current departure.
6. Usable capacity solves the problem of counting reserved spaces as though ordinary parcels could occupy them.
7. Queue ratio solves the problem of comparing demand on trams that have different usable capacities.
8. Balanced transfer solves the problem of leaving one tram overburdened while another eligible tram has room for its parcels.
9. Queue ratio depends on usable capacity because reserved spaces must be removed before available space can be used as the denominator.
10. A larger queue does not necessarily mean a larger queue ratio because the tram with more waiting parcels may also have substantially more usable capacity.
11. Two trams are eligible for balanced transfer only when their waiting parcels share a destination and delivery deadline and neither tram has departed.
12. A balanced transfer changes the distribution of waiting parcels but does not change their combined number or either tram's usable capacity.
13. After each whole-parcel transfer, both queue ratios must be recalculated before deciding whether another transfer would improve the balance.
14. `[agent-only]` Simulator regression checks reject ratio differences greater than 0.000001 from their stored reference results.
15. `[agent-only]` The Vesk Operations team publishes simulator reference tables on the first Monday of each quarter.
16. In the Copper Loop scenario, tram Nemi has eight total spaces and two reserved spaces, while tram Pavo has sixteen total spaces and four reserved spaces.
17. In the Copper Loop scenario, Nemi has twelve waiting ordinary parcels and Pavo has six, and all eighteen parcels share a destination and delivery deadline before either tram departs.
18. In the Copper Loop scenario, transferring six parcels from Nemi to Pavo leaves queues of six and twelve respectively, giving both trams a queue ratio of one.
